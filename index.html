Architectural Taxonomy

9 Core Concurrency Paradigms

How AI models attempted to evolve beyond linear rendezvous and lock-step barriers.

1. Lock-step PubSub & Barrier Rendezvous

Zero-allocation synchronous handoff where sender blocks until all registered subscribers acknowledge. Evolved with phase-alternating generations (PhasePubSub, GenPubSub) and atomic acknowledgement planes (ChanPubSub57DeepSeek).

2. Synchronous Rendezvous Casters

Dynamic subscriber registries with explicit registration/receive contracts and safe mid-flight withdrawal (ChanCaster, SeqCaster, SenseCaster). Zero-allocation fast-paths achieve sub-microsecond latency.

3. Bounded Lossy & Lossless Rings

Sequence-stamped ring buffers with atomic cursors (RingPubSub, RingCaster55Haiku, PhantomCast). Eliminates sender blocking while allowing subscribers to catch up or detect overruns.

4. Epoch Gates & Generation Signals

Parity-flipped generation markers and channel-close fan-out (EpochBroadcast, EpochTape, EpochCaster). Wakes unbounded subscribers with O(1) control-plane operations.

5. Nonblocking Mailbox & RCU Fanout

Copy-on-write subscriber arrays paired with per-consumer unbounded FIFO queues (QueuedFanout24Meta, SelectFanout, Fanout56MaiCode). Guarantees sender non-blocking at the cost of heap allocations.

6. Work Distribution / Competing Consumers

Distributes each item to exactly one receiver (ChanScatter, BaselineScatterQueue, StableRing32Nano). Safe mid-flight withdrawal prevents item loss when a consumer cancels.

7. Signal Gathering & State Beacons

Inverted fan-in signal collection (ChanGatherer) and loss-tracking atomic state beacons (ChanBeacon, RingBeacon).

8. Symmetric Pairwise Exchange

Two parties meet and swap one value each, with no sender or receiver role (SymExchanger72Gemini, BaselineExchanger). A cancelled party gets its own value back; a value is never duplicated or lost.

9. Deadline-Bounded Barrier Broadcast

A send waits for every member, but only until a deadline, and reports who did not acknowledge (Cohort73Meta, Fanout74Deepseek, PhaseCaster72Gemini, BaselineDeadlineBarrier). One stalled member delays a send by at most the deadline.

Failure Mode Analysis

Pathology Lab: Concurrency Traps in AI Code

Testing AI code under rigorous stress, race-detection, and fuzzing uncovered fundamental failure patterns.

1. Concurrent Mid-Flight Withdrawal Races

When a receiver abandons a select midway through a publication round, models frequently reused channel slots or completed a stale rendezvous, causing data races or double-deliveries.

2. Deadlock on Unbuffered Hub Teardown

Calling Close() while a publisher or subscriber was blocked on an unbuffered rendezvous channel routinely deadlocked because the teardown routine waited for active rounds to complete.

3. Hidden Lock Contention in "Non-Blocking" Paths

Several designs claimed lock-free non-blocking operation, but placed sync.Mutex inside TrySend(), causing catastrophic throughput collapse under 64-subscriber fan-out.

4. Silent Message Loss in Ring Buffers

Ring buffer outputs frequently failed to stamp items with monotonic sequences, causing slow subscribers to read torn values or skip laps without ever noticing they fell behind.

5. Zero-Allocation Illusions

Several responses claim "0 allocations per send" and allocate when measured: a per-publish node or notification channel, interface boxing (any), or a slice header that escapes. The tracker (01-readme.md) records each claim that the allocation tests contradict.
